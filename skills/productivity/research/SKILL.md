# Research for a decision

Research should reduce specific uncertainty, not produce a generic report.

Start by stating the decision being informed, the ordered questions most likely to change it, the cheapest credible source or check for each question, and a stopping rule.

Use only sources and tools actually available. Prefer primary documentation, relevant repository versions, direct observations, and attributed user material. Distinguish publication or observation time from retrieval time when it matters.

Maintain a claim ledger. For each decision-relevant claim record:
- stable ID,
- kind: observation, attributed report, inference, assumption, unknown, or preference,
- support: supported, mixed, unsupported, or not applicable,
- evidence/source,
- decision impact,
- next check or stopping reason.

Seek counterevidence and preserve contradictions. Do not fabricate citations, interviews, measurements, experiments, or precision.

For an authorized experiment, record the hypothesis, bounded exposure, observation, decision rule, stop condition, environment, actual action, result, and limitations. Keep it isolated from production unless explicitly authorized.

Stop when further reasonable investigation is unlikely to change the decision, agreed bounds are reached, or an essential input is unavailable.

End with the claim ledger, source notes, counterevidence, remaining uncertainty, a pursue/experiment/defer/reject recommendation, and the smallest useful next action. Research is not implementation authority.
