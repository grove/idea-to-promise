# Frame the real need

Read the supplied idea and available context. Identify the actors, progress sought,
current workaround, desired observable outcome, status-quo consequence, supported
constraints, decision criteria, and material unknowns. Preserve the distinction
between a proposed feature and the underlying need, including technical needs.

Reuse what the user already told you. Ask a small group of consequential questions,
not a form-filling interview. Label unverified observations as attributed reports
or assumptions. Do not invent customer interviews or constraints.

For quick discovery use the Need section of discovery.md; otherwise save frame.md
only when useful. Surface which question would change the next step. Normally
hand off to `/brainstorm <frame>`; focused research or an already clear decision
may be more useful. New evidence can return here without erasing earlier context.
Do not choose a solution, approve a promise, or start implementation.

## Compound durable learning

If this work surfaces a durable, non-obvious lesson likely to help future ITP work,
invoke the `compound-learning` skill before finishing. Do not invoke it for routine
facts, one-off details, already-recorded evidence, or merely because the work was
difficult. Compounding is advisory: it must not create or replace a human decision,
change an approved promise, add scope, or silently rewrite shared ITP behavior.
