# Discovery protocol

Idea to Promise turns an uncertain idea into a researched, explicit source promise worth making.

## Boundary

Idea to Promise answers **what should we promise, and why?** Promise to Proof answers **what exactly would make that promise true, can we implement it faithfully, and can we prove it?**

The boundary is deliberate:

```text
idea → framing → alternatives → research → decision → source promise
                                                    ↓
                                             Promise to Proof
```

Idea to Promise must not create P2P acceptance matrices, proof verdicts, candidate identities, or implementation plans.

## Evidence discipline

Research is decision-directed. Investigate the smallest set of questions likely to change the decision.

A useful claim ledger records:
- claim ID,
- claim kind,
- support state,
- evidence/source,
- decision impact,
- next check or stopping reason.

Distinguish observations, attributed reports, inferences, assumptions, unknowns, and preferences. Seek counterevidence. Do not fabricate citations, interviews, measurements, or experiments.

## Human decision

Keep recommendation, human choice, and approval of exact promise wording separate.

A selected direction is not automatically an approved promise. A saved file is not approval. **AGREED** requires attributable human approval of the exact source text.

Discovery may end in:
- pursue,
- bounded experiment,
- defer,
- reject/no-build.

A no-build result is valid and should not manufacture a promise merely to continue the workflow.

## Promise shape

A source promise should contain the observable intended outcome, promised behavior, boundaries/non-goals, binding constraints, and visible remaining assumptions. Research and rationale should remain advisory unless explicitly adopted as binding.

For an experiment, promise the bounded learning activity and decision rule—not the hoped-for result.

## Handoff

Once the exact source promise is agreed, hand it to Promise to Proof with:

```text
/plan-acceptance <agreed-source-path>
```

P2P retains its own planning, approval, implementation, review, and proof rules. Source approval does not itself authorize implementation or publication.
