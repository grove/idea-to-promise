# A feature request that targets the wrong problem

> Fictional teaching example. The dialogue and source notes are authored fixtures, not live-agent output, customer research, or real approval.

## Seed

**User:** “We should add an AI summary to every weekly account report.”

## Frame and opportunities

In this fictional brief S1, account managers spend time preparing reports because
customer names do not match across two internal lists. The brief contains no
interviews or measured demand for summaries.

O1 is reconciling account identity. O2 is understanding long narrative histories,
but O2 is presently an assumption, not an observed problem.

## Alternatives and research

A1 adds summaries; A2 fixes account matching; A3 retains the manual process.
C1 is an attributed report from S1 about mismatches. C2, “summaries solve most
preparation effort,” is a high-criticality, unsupported assumption. Inspecting the
supplied fixture workflow reveals that reconciliation occurs before any narrative
is read. That weakens the proposed causal story; it does not prove a real product
metric. No external customer study is invented.

**Agent:** “The supplied problem is account matching, not reading long histories.
A summary feature might solve a different problem. Do you want to investigate
matching instead, or defer this idea?”

## Human decision

**User:** “Reject the summaries feature for now. Reopen it only if we observe
reading history becoming a significant part of the job.”

D1 records reject, the actual scripted statement, the unsupported summary
assumption, and that revisit trigger. A separate self-check finds no need to
manufacture a new commitment to account-matching work: that is another idea, not
automatically approved scope.

## End

NO PROMISE. No specs, approval receipt or implementation tasks are created.
The useful result is a reasoned decision not to build this feature—not a polished
promise for something nobody actually needs.
